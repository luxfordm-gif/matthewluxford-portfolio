Reps.

A training app I designed and built for myself. Started as a way to stop wrestling with a PDF and a Google Doc every morning — turned into something I now use every single day.

Role
Design & build
Timeline
2026 – Present
Scope
Product · Web app · AI · Supabase

The backbone.

The gym's been my thing for a long time. Properly into it — bodybuilding for years, half-marathons in between, rugby at a decent league level, plenty of road cycling more recently. When the kids came along I doubled down. The point wasn't the medals or the PBs. It was being the kind of dad who shows up to the thing he says is important.

The gym is the one I'll always make time for. Four years of working with a personal coach, on and off. And in those four years, the one thing that's never stopped grinding on me is the tooling.

Designed it. Built it.
Train with it.

No brief. No client. Just the app I always wished existed — built around how I actually train, not how someone in a strategy deck thinks people train.

The annoyance.

Every block of training starts the same way. A PDF lands in my inbox. Then the dance begins.

Copy the PDF into a Google Doc. Tidy the columns. Add a date row. Each session, scroll back to last week, copy the column, paste it across, change the number. Forget to update the date. Misalign a row. Lose track of which set you're on. Try to spot progress on a screen the size of a beer mat.

Every two or three months a new plan comes in. The whole thing starts over.

AI has made bits of it easier. It still wasn't good.

So I built it.

A web app. Lives on my phone like a native one — saved to the home screen, opens in a tap. New plan arrives, I upload the PDF or CSV, and the app reads it, splits it into days, and lays the week out properly.

Then it gets out of the way.

I've spent enough years inside gym apps to know what I don't want from one. Most of them feel like they were built by someone who's never actually trained. Too rigid. No drop sets without three taps and a menu. Supersets buried. You can't just do the workout.

So I built every flexibility I've ever missed:

  • Drop sets, supersets, rest-pause — first-class, not workarounds
  • Edit anything mid-set — reps, weight, swap an exercise, add one in
  • Last-week glance — the number you're chasing, right there on the row
  • Big numbers, one thumb — readable mid-set, sweaty hands and all

It's an app built by someone who actually goes to the gym. Imagine that.

How I build it.

In the gym, I take voice notes between sets. What's working. What's annoying me. What's broken. Sometimes a half-formed feature, sometimes "why is this button so small."

Get home, day job kicks in. In the gaps, I export the notes into Claude, hand it the running list, and let it work through fixes while I'm on calls. Design in Figma, design back, into Claude Code, wire it into Supabase, make sure the SQL holds up.

The loop's tight: train, note, ship, train again. Most days the app I train with at 6am is a bit better than the one I trained with the day before.

It's been the most enjoyable side quest I've done in years. Designer into Claude into designer into code into database — and the thing at the end of it is something I actually open every morning.

Where it's going.

Right now it's mine. Works for me, fits how I train, solves the thing that's been annoying me for years.

The plan is to open it up. Build a proper site around it. A free tier that covers the basics — log a workout, track a plan, see your progress. A paid tier (subscription or one-off) for the advanced stuff: PDF/CSV import, custom set structures, longer-term progress views, anything else my voice notes keep flagging.

No timeline. No pressure. It earns its keep the second I open it each morning — anything beyond that is upside.
